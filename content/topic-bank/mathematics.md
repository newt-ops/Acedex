# Cross-Grade Secondary Mathematics Topic Map (Grades 9–12)

---

## 1. Complete Topic Inventory

Across the four Ethiopian secondary mathematics textbooks, the curriculum is organized into twenty-nine units across seven broad mathematical families:

- **Family 1: Foundations, Number Systems & Discrete Mathematics** (Sets, Number Systems, Sequences & Series, Business Applications)
- **Family 2: Algebra & Linear Systems** (Equations, Inequalities, Matrices, Determinants, Linear Programming)
- **Family 3: Relations & Functions** (Foundations, Polynomials, Exponentials, Logarithms, Inverses, Special Types, Rational Functions)
- **Family 4: Euclidean & Solid Geometry** (Polygons, Congruency & Similarity, Circles, Solid Figures)
- **Family 5: Coordinate Geometry, Transformations & Vectors** (Coordinate Geometry, Transformations of the Plane, Vectors in 2D & 3D)
- **Family 6: Trigonometry** (Trigonometric Ratios, Circular Functions, Identities, Equations, Radian Measure)
- **Family 7: Calculus, Statistics & Probability** (Differential & Integral Calculus, Descriptive/Inferential Statistics, Probability & Counting)

### 1. The Algebra-to-Calculus Function Pipeline

- **Grade 9 (Number Systems, Exponents & Absolute Values)**: Introduces integer and rational exponent laws (\\(a^n \cdot a^m = a^{n+m}\\), \\(a^{1/n} = \sqrt[n]{a}\\)) [484–487] and the algebraic definition of absolute value \\(|x|\\) as distance from the origin on a 1D number line [480–481].
- **Grade 10 (Polynomial, Exponential & Logarithmic Functions)**: Formalizes functions from relations [23–24]. Exponents are generalized into continuous exponential functions \\(f(x) = a^x\\) with horizontal asymptote \\(y=0\\) [98–99], paired with their inverses, logarithmic functions \\(f(x) = \log_a x\\) [100–104]. Polynomials are explored via degree, division, factor/remainder theorems, and end behavior [51–85].
- **Grade 11 (Power, Piecewise & Rational Functions)**: Rational exponents become power functions \\(f(x) = x^{m/n}\\) and \\(x^{-m/n}\\), with comprehensive domain/range rules [186–199]. Absolute value expands into the modulus function \\(f(x) = |x|\\), signum function \\(\text{sgn}(x)\\), and greatest integer (floor) function \\(\lfloor x \rfloor\\) [200–203]. Polynomial factoring directly enables the analysis of rational functions \\(f(x) = \frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\\), vertical/horizontal/oblique asymptotes, and removable discontinuities [211–222].
- **Grade 12 (Differential & Integral Calculus)**: These function families become the objects of dynamic analysis. The power rule \\(\frac{d}{dx}[x^n] = n x^{n-1}\\) and integration rule \\(\int x^n dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + C\\) operate across the polynomial and power functions studied in Grades 10–11. Rational functions motivate partial fraction decomposition before integration. Points of non-differentiability (such as the sharp corner of \\(|x|\\) at \\(x=0\\)) are evaluated using Grade 11 piecewise definitions.

### 2. Linear Systems, Matrices, and Optimization

- **Grade 9 (Equations & Inequalities)**: Students solve systems of two linear equations in two variables using substitution, elimination, and line-intersection graphing [514–520]. Linear inequalities in two variables establish the concept of half-planes [531–535].
- **Grade 10 (Coordinate Geometry)**: Lines are formalized using gradients (\\(m = \frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1} = \tan \theta\\)), slope-intercept form (\\(y = mx + b\\)), and conditions for parallelism (\\(m_1 = m_2\\)) and perpendicularity (\\(m_1 m_2 = -1\\)) [156–161].
- **Grade 11 (Matrices & Determinants)**: Grade 9 linear systems are abstracted into matrix equations \\(AX = B\\). Systems of 2 and 3 variables are solved using Gaussian elimination on augmented matrices (Row Echelon Form) [225–228, 248–251], matrix inversion via cofactors (\\(X = A^{-1}B\\)) [247, 261–262], and Cramer's Rule via determinants [261–263].
- **Grade 12 (Linear Programming)**: Merges Grade 9 half-plane inequalities, Grade 10 line graphing, and matrix constraint concepts. Students optimize linear objective functions \\(Z = ax + by\\) across polygonal convex feasible regions using the Corner-Point Theorem [402–414].

### 3. Trigonometric Evolution: Geometric Ratios to Dynamic Functions

- **Grade 9 (Right-Angled Triangle Geometry)**: Trigonometry is restricted to static side ratios (\\(\sin \theta = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}\\), \\(\cos \theta = \frac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}\\), \\(\tan \theta = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}\\)) for acute angles \\(\theta \in (0^\circ, 90^\circ)\\) in right triangles [545–547].
- **Grade 10 (Circular Functions & Identities)**: Angles are generalized beyond \\(90^\circ\\) to all real numbers using radian measure (\\(s = r\theta\\)) and terminal rays intersecting the unit circle \\(x^2 + y^2 = 1\\) [117–120]. Trigonometric ratios become periodic functions (\\(2\pi\\) for sine/cosine, \\(\pi\\) for tangent) with reference angles and wave graphs [120–127]. Grade 9's Pythagoras theorem is transformed into the fundamental identity \\(\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1\\) [128–129].
- **Grade 11 (Vector Cross Products & Rotations)**: Trigonometric values are applied to calculate vector dot products (\\(\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = |\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|\cos \theta\\)) and cross products (\\(|\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v}| = |\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|\sin \theta\\)). Plane transformation equations utilize rotation matrices driven by sine and cosine of angle \\(\theta\\) [286–289].
- **Grade 12 (Calculus and Kinematics)**: The gradient of a curve's tangent line is interpreted as \\(m = \tan \theta\\), directly connecting differential rates of change to the trigonometric slope of the tangent line.

### 4. Vector Geometry and Transformations

- **Grade 9 (Vectors in 2D)**: Introduces vectors as directed line segments with magnitude and direction, governed geometrically by the Triangle Law and Parallelogram Law, with component representations \\(\vec{v} = (x, y)\\) [573–576].
- **Grade 11 (Vectors in 3D & Vector Equations)**: Extends vectors into 3D coordinate space using standard basis vectors \\(\mathbf{i}, \mathbf{j}, \mathbf{k}\\) [269–276]. Introduces the inner/dot product for work and orthogonality (\\(\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0\\)) [271–274], and the vector/cross product for torque and spatial areas [277–280]. Vectors are used to formulate equations of lines and tangent lines to circles [280–283].
- **Grade 11 (Transformations of the Plane)**: Direct geometric application of vectors as translation operators \\((x, y) \mapsto (x + a, y + b)\\), along with reflections across lines and rotations about pivot points [284–288].

### 5. Measurement, Area, and Integral Accumulation

- **Grade 9 (Plane Polygons)**: Establishes formulas for interior angles, apothems, perimeters, and areas of regular polygons, as well as area ratios of similar polygons (\\(k^2\\)) [553–558, 568–570].
- **Grade 10 (Circles & Composed Solids)**: Broadens plane area to circular sectors and segments (\\(A = \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2 - \frac{1}{2}r^2 \sin \theta\\)). Extends geometric measurement to three dimensions: surface areas and volumes of prisms, cylinders, pyramids, cones, spheres, frustums, and composite solids [139–155].
- **Grade 12 (Integral Calculus)**: Generalizes polygonal and circular area calculations to arbitrary continuous regions. The curriculum constructs Riemann sums by partitioning intervals into \\(n\\) subintervals of width \\(\Delta x = \frac{b-a}{n}\\) [382–384]. The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus establishes that exact bounded area is computed by evaluating the anti-derivative at the boundary points: \\(A = \int_a^b f(x) dx = F(b) - F(a)\\) [386–390].

### 6. Statistics, Counting Principles, and Probability

- **Grade 9 (Descriptive Statistics & Experimental Probability)**: Covers data collection, frequency distribution tables, histograms, bar charts, mean/median/mode of ungrouped data, range, variance, and standard deviation [584–599]. Probability is introduced through trials, coin/dice experiments, sample spaces, and simple theoretical ratios [600–605].
- **Grade 11 (Grouped Statistics & Combinatorial Probability)**: Statistics expands to grouped continuous data with class intervals, class boundaries, frequency polygons, and cumulative ogives [291–292]. Introduces measures of location: grouped mean, median, quartiles (\\(Q_1, Q_2, Q_3\\)), deciles (\\(D_k\\)), and percentiles (\\(P_k\\)) [293–296]. Probability is placed on a combinatorial foundation: Fundamental Counting Principles, permutations \\(P(n, r)\\), combinations \\(C(n, r)\\), and the Binomial Theorem [300–304].
- **Grade 12 (Advanced Dispersion, Skewness & Sampling)**: Re-evaluates dispersion by introducing absolute measures (Mean Deviation, Quartile Deviation) and relative measures (Coefficient of Variation, Coefficient of Mean/Quartile Deviation) [397–400]. Introduces frequency curve morphology (skewness, symmetry) and scientific sampling methodology (Random: simple, stratified, cluster, systematic; Non-random).

### Phase I: Foundational Core (Modules 1 & 2)

#### Module 1: Mathematical Foundations, Number Systems & Set Theory

- **Textbook Units to Study**:
  - Grade 9, Unit 1: Further on Sets (pp. 1–26).
  - Grade 9, Unit 2: The Number System (pp. 27–104) [441–442].
- **Why Study Together**: Set builder notation, Venn diagrams, set operations, and interval notation form the primary language used to define function domains, matrix sets, and sample spaces throughout Grades 10–12. Rational and irrational numbers, laws of exponents, and radical simplifications are direct prerequisites for power, exponential, and polynomial expressions.
- **Mastery Checkpoint**: Can you use Euclid's division lemma, perform operations on radicals, manipulate fractional exponents (\\(a^{m/n}\\)), and express solution intervals using rigorous set notation?

#### Module 2: Core Algebra, Equations & Inequalities

- **Textbook Units to Study**:
  - Grade 9, Unit 3: Solving Equations (pp. 105–150).
  - Grade 9, Unit 4: Solving Inequalities (pp. 151–184).
- **Why Study Together**: Equations and inequalities are parallel mathematical structures. Mastering linear systems, absolute value equations/inequalities, quadratic factoring, completing the square, the quadratic formula, and sign chart techniques provides the algebraic machinery required for analyzing curve behavior and coordinate regions.
- **Mastery Checkpoint**: Can you solve absolute value inequalities (\\(|ax+b| < c\\)), factor non-monic quadratic expressions, and construct 1D sign charts for quadratic inequalities (\\(ax^2 + bx + c \ge 0\\))?

---

### Phase II: Structural Branches (Modules 3, 4 & 5)

#### Module 3: Linear Systems, Coordinate Geometry, Matrices & Linear Programming

- **Textbook Units to Study**:
  - Grade 10, Unit 7: Coordinate Geometry (pp. 345–380).
  - Grade 11, Unit 3: Matrices (pp. 131–206).
  - Grade 11, Unit 4: Determinants and Their Properties (pp. 207–250).
  - Grade 12, Unit 4: Introduction to Linear Programming (pp. 237–302).
- **Why Study Together**: This module traces linear relationships from single lines to multi-variable optimization. Grade 10 provides line equations and slopes; Grade 11 shows how to represent and solve 2- and 3-variable linear systems using augmented matrices, row echelon reductions, matrix inverses, and Cramer's rule; Grade 12 takes those same linear systems and half-planes to optimize objective functions over convex polygonal feasible regions.
- **Mastery Checkpoint**: Can you reduce a \\(3 \times 4\\) augmented matrix to solve a system, evaluate a \\(3 \times 3\\) determinant via cofactors, and locate the optimal corner point of a linear programming polytope?

#### Module 4: The Comprehensive Function Hierarchy

- **Textbook Units to Study**:
  - Grade 10, Unit 1: Relations and Functions (pp. 1–66).
  - Grade 10, Unit 2: Polynomial Functions (pp. 67–120) [8–9].
  - Grade 10, Unit 3: Exponential and Logarithmic Functions (pp. 121–198).
  - Grade 11, Unit 1: Relations and Functions (pp. 1–82).
  - Grade 11, Unit 2: Rational Expressions and Rational Functions (pp. 83–130).
- **Why Study Together**: This is the central conceptual engine of high school mathematics. Combining these units allows you to study functions as a unified taxonomy:
  1. Foundations (domain, range, vertical line test, injective/bijective classifications).
  2. Algebraic families (linear, quadratic, higher-degree polynomials via division/remainder/factor theorems and rational zero tests).
  3. Transcendental families (exponential and logarithmic functions as mutual reflections across \\(y = x\\)).
  4. Structural operations (inverses \\(f^{-1}\\), composition \\(g \circ f\\)).
  5. Special real functions (power functions \\(x^r\\), modulus \\(|x|\\), signum \\(\text{sgn}(x)\\), floor \\(\lfloor x \rfloor\\)).
  6. Rational functions (asymptotes, holes, and partial fraction decomposition).
- **Mastery Checkpoint**: Can you derive \\(f^{-1}(x)\\), compute the domain of \\((g \circ f)(x)\\), factor a quartic polynomial using the Rational Zero Test, and find all vertical, horizontal, and oblique asymptotes of a rational function?

#### Module 5: Euclidean, Solid & Metric Geometry

- **Textbook Units to Study**:
  - Grade 9, Unit 6: Regular Polygons (pp. 207–238).
  - Grade 9, Unit 7: Congruency and Similarity (pp. 239–276) [444–445].
  - Grade 10, Unit 5: Circles (pp. 251–286).
  - Grade 10, Unit 6: Solid Figures (pp. 287–344) [10–11].
- **Why Study Together**: Unifies plane and spatial geometry. Similarity criteria and perimeter/area ratios (\\(k, k^2\\)) directly support circle sector and segment calculations. Solid geometry then builds on these 2D foundations to calculate surface areas and volumes of prisms, pyramids, cylinders, cones, spheres, frustums, and composite solids.
- **Mastery Checkpoint**: Can you apply intersecting chord/secant theorems, calculate the area of a circle segment, determine frustum volume, and set up geometric similarity proofs?

---

### Phase III: Advanced Analytical Branches (Modules 6, 7 & 8)

#### Module 6: The Complete Trigonometry Pipeline

- **Textbook Units to Study**:
  - Grade 9, Unit 5: Introduction to Trigonometry (pp. 185–206).
  - Grade 10, Unit 4: Trigonometric Functions (pp. 199–250).
- **Why Study Together**: Studying these two units back-to-back bridges the geometric and functional views of trigonometry. You progress seamlessly from right-triangle side ratios for acute angles to circular functions on the unit circle across all real angles, radian conversions (\\(s = r\theta\\)), wave graphing (amplitude, period, phase), and fundamental Pythagorean identities.
- **Mastery Checkpoint**: Can you convert between degrees and radians, find reference angles in any quadrant, prove identities using \\(\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1\\), and sketch transformed sine and cosine graphs?

#### Module 7: Vectors, Analytic Geometry & Transformations

- **Textbook Units to Study**:
  - Grade 9, Unit 8: Vectors in Two Dimensions (pp. 277–310).
  - Grade 11, Unit 5: Vectors (pp. 251–312).
  - Grade 11, Unit 6: Transformations of the Plane (pp. 313–354).
- **Why Study Together**: Connects vector algebra with coordinate geometry. Beginning with 2D geometric vectors and components, you advance to 3D space vectors (\\(\mathbf{i}, \mathbf{j}, \mathbf{k}\\)), calculate dot products for work and orthogonality, evaluate cross products for torque and area, write vector line equations, and apply vectors as transformation operators (translations, reflections, rotations).
- **Mastery Checkpoint**: Can you compute \\(\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}\\) and \\(\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v}\\), find the angle between two 3D vectors, write parametric equations of lines, and find the image of a line or circle under plane reflection or rotation?

#### Module 8: Differential & Integral Calculus

- **Textbook Units to Study**:
  - Grade 12, Unit 2: Introduction to Calculus (pp. 61–158) [308–309].
- **Prerequisites Required**: Modules 1, 2, 4, and 6.
- **Why Study Here**: Calculus is the culmination of high school function analysis. Having mastered polynomials, rational functions, power functions, absolute values, and trigonometry in Modules 4 and 6, you can investigate their dynamic behavior: secant-to-tangent limits, derivative rules (power, product, quotient, chain), curve monotonicity, critical numbers, and optimization via the First Derivative Test. You then invert the process through anti-differentiation, compute Riemann sums, and evaluate definite integrals to find areas bounded under curves.
- **Mastery Checkpoint**: Can you find the tangent and normal line equations to a curve, identify local extrema using the First Derivative Test, and calculate the exact area under a curve using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus?

---

### Phase IV: Discrete, Financial & Stochastic Systems (Modules 9 & 10)

#### Module 9: Discrete Mathematics, Sequences & Financial Modeling

- **Textbook Units to Study**:
  - Grade 12, Unit 1: Sequences and Series (pp. 1–60).
  - Grade 12, Unit 5: Mathematical Applications in Business (pp. 303–416).
- **Why Study Together**: Sequences and series represent discrete functions defined over \\(\mathbb{Z}^+\\). Arithmetic and geometric sequences, partial sums, sigma notation, and infinite geometric series provide the direct mathematical foundation for financial mathematics, including compound interest, continuous compounding (\\(e^{rt}\\)), annuities, loan amortizations, and asset depreciation.
- **Mastery Checkpoint**: Can you determine the convergence of an infinite geometric series, convert mixed recurring decimals to fractions, compute future/present value annuities, and construct loan amortization schedules?

#### Module 10: Complete Statistics, Counting & Probability Theory

- **Textbook Units to Study**:
  - Grade 9, Unit 9: Statistics and Probability (pp. 311–360).
  - Grade 11, Unit 7: Statistics (pp. 355–412).
  - Grade 11, Unit 8: Probability (pp. 413–480).
  - Grade 12, Unit 3: Statistics (pp. 159–236).
- **Why Study Together**: Grouping these four units creates an unbroken learning path from basic descriptive statistics and empirical experiments to advanced statistical analysis and combinatorial probability:
  1. Data collection, organization, histograms, and basic central tendency/dispersion (Grade 9).
  2. Grouped continuous data, frequency polygons, ogives, and positional measures (\\(Q_k, D_k, P_k\\)) (Grade 11).
  3. Rigorous probability theory via Fundamental Counting Principles, permutations, combinations, and the Binomial Theorem (Grade 11).
  4. Advanced absolute and relative dispersion (Mean Deviation, Quartile Deviation, Coefficient of Variation), distribution skewness, and scientific sampling methodology (Grade 12).
- **Mastery Checkpoint**: Can you calculate quartiles and percentiles from a grouped frequency table, compute probabilities using permutations/combinations, determine the Coefficient of Variation to compare dataset stability, and identify skewness from frequency curves?

### Summary Checklist for Topic-Based Study

To implement this study system:

1. **Follow the module order (1 through 10)**: This guarantees that every prerequisite is mastered before it is needed in later applications.
2. **Consult specific textbook page numbers**: Use the page references provided in Section 1 to read definitions, study worked examples, and complete corresponding exercises.
3. **Verify connections across grades**: When a module combines units from different grades (such as Module 3 combining Grades 10, 11, and 12, or Module 4 combining Grades 10 and 11), pay special attention to how notation becomes more abstract and how earlier techniques are generalized.
